# What happened, 2026-08-19

A brief written for the rename sweep invoked the clause that lets a session edit a register **in the
same change that renames what that register points at**, and treated the sweep of `index.md`,
`sprint.md` and `log.md` as an exercise of it ([[the-one-writer-rule-covers-the-registers-too]]).

**The librarian established that there was nothing to license.** Those three files are the
librarian's to write outright — the clause exists for a *session* editing a register, and the writer
here was the librarian. **No divergence was recorded, because none occurred.**

# Why a citation that was not needed is worth a page

**A cited licence reads as a precedent whether or not it was required.** The next session greps for
how a register was edited last time, finds a brief invoking an exception, and concludes that editing
a register is a thing sessions do with an exception in hand. The rule then erodes by imitation rather
than by anyone deciding to weaken it — which is the mechanism every recorded divergence in this
project began with ([[ask-before-weakening-or-working-around-a-rule]]).

**The over-claim is the cheap direction and that is exactly why it spreads.** Citing an exception you
do not need costs nothing at the time and cannot be caught by any gate: the change is legal, the
paperwork is thorough, and the record is wrong only about *why*.

# The shape

**Recording a permission you did not use is a form of the same defect as using one you did not
record.** Both leave the log saying something the tree does not support, and only one of them looks
careless. This store's enforcement is git plus the log ([[the-librarian-writes-unattended]]), so a
log entry that overstates the rule's reach damages the only instrument there is.

**The correction is naming it, not deleting it.** The brief stands; this page is what stops it being
read as authority.
