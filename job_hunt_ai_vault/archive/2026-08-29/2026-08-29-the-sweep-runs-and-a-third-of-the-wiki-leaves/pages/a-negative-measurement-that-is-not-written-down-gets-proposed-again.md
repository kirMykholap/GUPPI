# The incident, 2026-08-26

**A session built a caveat about ordering on [[the-suite-gets-a-scanner-exclusion-before-a-narrowed-gate]]**,
which still reads *measure first, then narrow*.

**The owner said the measurement had already been done and had given nothing.** *(His own words are
not in the journal — the session recorded its understanding in English, so this is a paraphrase and
is not signed as a quotation* — [[a-block-attributed-to-the-owner-is-written-in-his-own-russian]].*)*

**The result is on no page.** So the caveat was false, and the next session would have proposed the
measurement a third time.

# Why a negative result is the one most likely to go unwritten

**A positive measurement produces a number somebody wants to cite.** A negative one produces nothing
to carry — the thing did not move — so there is no artefact whose existence reminds anyone to file
it ([[a-brief-that-carries-a-cost-without-its-findings-suppresses-the-finding]]).

**And the proposal that asked for it stays standing**, with its ordering intact, which reads to the
next reader as *this has not been tried*.

# What the page actually said, and where the gap is

**The proposal's own record of 2026-08-24 says the measurement failed** — three runs at 25.3, 16.2
and 19.3 seconds, a spread larger than the effect, and the wrong subject besides. **What it does not
say is that the owner considers the question closed by that.**

**A failed measurement and a settled negative are different states**, and the page carries only the
first ([[a-proposal-must-be-rechecked-when-the-frame-changes]]).

# What follows

**File the negative in the turn it is heard**, as a `measure:` line, with what it does and does not
mean. It is the only class of result whose absence looks exactly like it was never tried.
