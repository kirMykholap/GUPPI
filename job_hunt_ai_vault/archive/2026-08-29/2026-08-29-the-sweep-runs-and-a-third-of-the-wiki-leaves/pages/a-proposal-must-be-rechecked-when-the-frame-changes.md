# The session proposed cutting the only working example of the mechanism it was defending

The `batches` → `collection` merge looked right under the frame *make the dictionary smaller*, and
it was proposed **in the same session that replaced that frame** with *does the tag narrow inside a
folder* ([[the-folder-is-the-first-narrowing]]).

Under the new frame `batches` is a **refinement** of `collection` — 18 pages narrowed to 5 — which
is precisely what the new rule is built on. The session was arguing for refinements and proposing
to delete one, in the same hour.

**It was caught only because the owner asked why.** Nothing in the process re-checks a live
proposal against a rule adopted after it was written: the proposal is a paragraph in a chat log or
a page in `proposals/`, and the new rule arrives somewhere else entirely.

## What to do with it, given there is no mechanism

- **When a frame changes, the proposals made under it are the first thing to re-read** — not the
  pages that implement the old frame, which are loud, but the intentions that were never executed,
  which are quiet.
- `proposals/` makes this cheaper than it was, because an intention is now a page with a folder
  rather than a clause inside something else ([[proposals-are-a-folder-not-a-status]]). It does not
  make it automatic: nothing links a proposal to the frame it was reasoned under.
