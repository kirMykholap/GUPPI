# The incident

**The criterion separating movable rules from unmovable ones was worked out from scratch and
reported as a finding** — while the corpus already held it, written and measured, on a page an agent
had been dispatched to read ([[moving-a-rule-into-claude-rules-with-a-paths-scope]],
[[a-rule-moving-to-conditional-delivery-needs-its-page-first]]).

**The cost is a round trip**, and a round trip here is the unit of expense: dispatch, read, report,
reconcile ([[agent-round-trip-cost]]). Nothing was wrong with the derivation — it agreed with the
page — which is what makes it invisible unless somebody compares.

# Why a derivation looks better than a lookup while it is happening

**Deriving produces an argument; looking up produces a citation.** The argument reads as work and
the citation reads as a formality, so a session under its own momentum prefers the first — and the
second is what the store exists to make possible ([[the-vault-is-compiled-not-retrieved]]).

**A derivation that agrees is not free.** It costs the round, and it produces a second copy of a
claim with no link between them, which is how two pages start drifting
([[a-copied-list-drifts-and-a-sweep-cannot]]).

# What to do instead

**Ask before deriving, not after** — that is what `/pre-task` is, and it surfaced constraints in
this same session that could not have been queried by name
([[a-pre-task-paid-for-itself-in-findings-nobody-would-have-made]]).

**And when a derivation is done anyway, reconcile it against the page rather than reporting it as
new.** The session that measures the same split a librarian derived separately gets a confirmation
worth having ([[a-subagents-most-believable-sentence-is-the-one-to-measure]]); the session that
reports it as a finding has bought the same sentence twice.
