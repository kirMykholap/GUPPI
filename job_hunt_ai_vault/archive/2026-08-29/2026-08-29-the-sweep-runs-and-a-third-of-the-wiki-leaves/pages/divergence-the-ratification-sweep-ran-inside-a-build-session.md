# What was broken

**[[the-ratification-sweep-waits-for-a-session-of-its-own]] ruled the sweep a session of its own**
and rejected *"doing it today, at the end of a session that had already spent itself. Blocks read
consecutively are read carelessly by the tenth."* The sweep ran inside a build session anyway.

**The rejected alternative is the one that page states: obey it, and bring the list on another day.**

# Why it looked unnecessary, which is the part worth keeping

**The owner routed the sweep to the retro himself**, to fill a thirty-four-minute run. Then it
happened earlier still, in ordinary conversation, and nobody named the departure at the moment it
was taken — the session had flagged it before it happened and let it pass two turns early without
flagging it again.

**Every recorded divergence in this project began with the rule looking unnecessary**, and this one
began with the owner's own scheduling making it look like a formality.

# The outcome, recorded with it and not instead of it

> **owner:** «мы отступили от решения, но уже как есть»

**Of the blocks shown, he rejected exactly one — the one with a drifted number in it.** The
divergence stands regardless: a good outcome does not retire an argument about conditions
([[a-question-asked-is-not-a-question-answered]]).

**And the decision was changed rather than merely broken**, by him, at the retro of the same day
([[a-sweep-may-run-inside-a-session-when-the-list-is-assembled-in-advance-and-shown-whole]]). The
change is his; this page is the record that the rule was broken first.
