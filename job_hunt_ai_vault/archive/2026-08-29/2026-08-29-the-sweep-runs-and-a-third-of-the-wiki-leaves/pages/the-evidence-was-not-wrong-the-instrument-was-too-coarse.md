# A plan with three days of evidence, refuted by one measurement taken a different way

The plan was to split `vault`, `process` and `testing` across the corpus. It carried three days of
counts behind it ([[dynamic-tag-classification-by-a-skill]]) and it was **refuted before it was
applied**, by measuring the same tags per folder instead of over the whole corpus.

**`testing` at `2036d98`: 5 of 47 pages in `decisions/` — ten percent, a tag doing its job — and 13
of 19 in `lessons/`.** The corpus-wide number could not express *healthy here, useless there*, and
the remedy it implied would have damaged the tag everywhere in order to fix one place.

## What is general in it

**The evidence was not wrong.** Every count taken over three days was correct. **The instrument was
too coarse to see what those counts were evidence of** — one number over a whole corpus cannot
carry a per-folder distribution, so a real signal arrived as a wrong conclusion.

That is a different failure from the ones already on record here, and it is worth telling apart
from them:

- [[a-measurement-agreeing-with-no-expectation-needs-a-different-instrument]] — the number was wrong
  and nothing contradicted it.
- [[a-stated-count-drifts-from-the-list-it-heads]] — the number was right when written and the list
  moved under it.
- **This one** — every number was right, and the *shape* of the measurement decided the conclusion.

**The tell is that the remedy was corpus-wide while the complaint was local.** A fix whose scope is
wider than the evidence that motivated it is a signal that the instrument, not the plan, is what
needs re-taking. What the re-measurement produced instead is
[[the-dense-tags-are-not-split-and-the-standing-advisories-are-named-here]] and
[[the-folder-is-the-first-narrowing]].
