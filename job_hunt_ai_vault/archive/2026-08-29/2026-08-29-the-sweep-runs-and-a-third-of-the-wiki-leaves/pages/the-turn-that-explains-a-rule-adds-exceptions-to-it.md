# The rule had to be applied to the session's own rewrites twice before it held

The rule was that a page title carries no measurable quantity
([[a-title-names-what-is-counted-instead-of-counting-it]]). **Both attempts to demonstrate it left
counts in the demonstration** — "one definition", "two moments" — and the owner caught both.

Neither was an oversight in the ordinary sense. Each was a *principled* exception offered in the
same breath as the rule: a count bound to a date is stable, a design invariant like *one* finaliser
is the claim rather than a measurement. Both are reasonable. Both were refused, and the rule is
unconditional as a result.

## Why the owner refused rather than accepted the reasonable version

**A rule with exceptions is a rule nobody can apply.** The reader of a title six months from now
has to decide whether this count is one of the permitted ones, which is exactly the judgement the
rule existed to remove. An unconditional rule is checkable by anyone; a rule with two carve-outs is
checkable only by whoever remembers them.

## The shape worth carrying

**The exceptions were being added by the same turn that was explaining the rule.** That is not
carelessness, it is what explaining does: an explanation reaches for the hard case, and the hard
case is where the exception looks most obviously right.

So the moment to distrust is not the moment a rule is proposed — it is the next turn, when it is
being illustrated. That is the same discipline the repository's `CLAUDE.md` states as *ask before
you weaken, remove or work around any rule*, and it is the second recorded instance of the pattern
that every divergence in this project began with the rule looking unnecessary.

## It recurred the next day, in a weaker form — found 2026-08-18

**This lesson was written on 2026-08-16 about the session that first stated the no-quantity rule.**
On 2026-08-17 the same shape returned, one level down: the turn that *built the check* for the rule
shipped an escape hatch with it.

**The weakening is what makes the recurrence interesting.** The two carve-outs the owner refused had
**stated boundaries** — counts bound to a date, and design invariants — and could be argued about.
What replaced them was **unbounded free text, judged by nobody**: any page could declare any reason
and the run went green ([[a-field-admitted-around-the-admission-procedure-outlives-the-rule-it-softens]]).

**An exception with a boundary can be refused. An exception with no boundary cannot even be
inspected** — which is why the second form survived a day longer than the first and had to be found
by a retrospective rather than by an argument
([[the-quantity-field-is-rejected-and-the-rule-stays-unconditional]]).
