# One rule, and it reads differently per type

- **An `entity` is a noun phrase** — "the wiki as docs storage". There the label *is* the answer:
  the page exists to say what the thing is, and a sentence would add nothing the reader has to know
  before opening it.
- **A `decision` or a `lesson` states its claim.** The search surface here is filenames
  ([[filenames-carry-no-category-prefix]], [[the-folder-is-the-first-narrowing]]), and a bare label
  in that position makes the result stop answering: a reader who greps and sees only a subject must
  open the page to learn whether it says yes or no.

Decided by the owner on 2026-08-16, alongside the rule that a title carries no measurable quantity
([[a-title-names-what-is-counted-instead-of-counting-it]]). The two are separate: that one says
what a title may not contain, this one says what shape the rest of it takes.

## It is one field of something larger that does not exist yet

**This is one of the fields a per-type schema would define.** The vault's `CLAUDE.md` describes one
page schema for every type, and that universality is what let two records be filed as things they
do not assert — [[a-schema-per-type-says-what-a-record-asserts]] carries that design, and it is not
taken.

## The form is bounded and the length is not — 2026-08-19

**This page says what shape a title takes; nothing says how long it may be, and that is now
decided rather than unsaid.** A decision stating its claim is longer than a label, and a claim that
names what it counts is longer again — the corpus paid that price deliberately, and the owner
refused a length limit in the check when it was offered
([[the-length-of-a-title-is-unbounded-and-no-check-measures-it]],
[[a-title-names-what-is-counted-instead-of-counting-it]]).
