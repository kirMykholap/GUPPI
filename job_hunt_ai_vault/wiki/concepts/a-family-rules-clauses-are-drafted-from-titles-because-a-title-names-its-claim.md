# The reading

**Every one of the fourteen prohibitions in the largest family is a complete declarative sentence**,
and all fourteen are about the `governs:` mechanism — the very thing the family rule replaces.

**That is not luck.** Rule 10 of `vault-check.sh` obliges a title to name what it claims rather than
count it, and the store has enforced it since 2026-08-18
([[a-title-names-what-is-counted-instead-of-counting-it]],
[[the-no-quantity-rule-reads-the-title-and-the-filename-and-never-the-body]]). A rule written to keep
numbers out of titles produced titles that carry claims, and the claim is what a rule clause needs.

**So a family rule is drafted from 320 titles read with their slugs in one 72 KB pass — about
eighteen thousand tokens — with no page opened at all.**

# What that gives up, named rather than discovered

**A title carries the claim and not the conditions.** *No new entity where an existing one can carry
the claim* is a complete sentence and says nothing about what counts as carrying.

**So each clause is a sentence plus a link to the decision it came from**, and the decision stays
reachable in `archive/`. The clause is the thing a reader is served; the decision is where the
conditions, the rejected alternative and the measurement stay
([[what-an-archived-page-asserts-is-frozen-and-how-it-is-found-is-live]]).

**And a family whose titles do not read as clauses is a finding rather than an obstacle** — it is how
the pass identifies families that were never one question.

# Why the pass is cheap and the verdict is not

**Reading is one command; deciding is 99 judgements.** The titles pass costs a fraction of a session
and produces a table, and the table is empty where it matters until the owner marks it
([[the-authority-register-groups-the-corpus-and-the-family-is-the-unit-of-a-cleanup]]).
