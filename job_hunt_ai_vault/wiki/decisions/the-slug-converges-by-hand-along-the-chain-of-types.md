# Context

**A slug shared across types became legal on 2026-08-30 and had no subject.** Rule 3 of the gate
compares slugs within a type; two pages of different types sharing one are legal and expected, and the
shared slug is the route a reader walks
([[authority-uniqueness-becomes-a-per-type-question]]). Measured that day: **zero slugs were shared
across types anywhere in `wiki/`.**

**The family rules made the permission load-bearing.** A rule compiled from a family inherits one
member decision's slug, and that decision leaves for `archive/` — so nothing live would carry the slug
and the hook would match nothing. The librarian moved the slug on fourteen of the nineteen pages onto
one a live concept or entity already carried.

**The session read the gate's refusal as an obstacle to that**, and put it to the owner as a problem to
be solved. It is not one.

# The question

**Is the target state one slug per question per type, converged by some mechanism — or one slug carried
along a chain of types, converged by hand?**

# How it works after

**One `authority:` slug is carried along a chain of types** — decision to proposal or rule to concept
or entity — and the slug propagates along it. **Rule 3 of the gate guards that** by refusing two pages
of one type sharing a slug, which is the intended behaviour rather than an obstacle to it: the types
beget one another and the gate watches that they do not duplicate.

**The convergence is done by hand, page by page, after the sweep.** The session finds the pages that
can be merged under one slug and repairs their `authority:`. **Rejected: anything mechanical.**

**It runs in the session after the one that ratified it**, over the roughly 245 live concepts,
entities and proposals, and not folded into the tail of the session that wrote the family rules.

**What that costs while it waits.** Four of the nineteen family rules answer for a family holding no
live concept or entity at all — `retro-ratification`, `page-naming`, `missing-decision-compilation`,
`repealed-status-mint` — so nothing in `wiki/` carries their slug and the hook serves them to nobody.
**Their coverage waits on a page being written, not on a slug being moved**, which is a different
piece of work from the convergence and is not scheduled by this decision.

# Recommendation

**By hand, and next session.** The session recommended against folding the pass into the tail of an
already long session, on the ground that judgements made tired are hurried ones, and he took it.

**Rejected: a mechanical convergence.** A script cannot tell that two slugs are the same question —
that is the judgement the register exists to record, and a wrong merge moves an address.

**Rejected: reading rule 3's refusal as something to work around.** It is what makes the shared slug
mean one question rather than two pages that happen to agree.

gate следит за тем, чтобы они не дублировались.»

**And on how it is done:** «Но мы проведем уборку вручную. То есть сами найдем страницы, которые можно
обьеденить под одним slug. Значит нам нужно будет вручную пройтись по страницам и исправить у них
authority после чистки.»

**And on when:** «в следующей».

# The measurement that sizes it, and the one it made false

**Fourteen of the nineteen rule pages written on 2026-08-31 carry a slug a live concept or entity also
carries**, so the lookup delivery serves something on the day it was built. The session had predicted
zero, on the correct reading that no slug was shared beforehand.

**So the sentence "zero slugs are shared across types anywhere in `wiki/`" went false the same week it
was written**, and it stands on [[authority-uniqueness-becomes-a-per-type-question]] where it was
measured. It is corrected there and dated rather than deleted — a number in prose goes false without
saying so ([[a-drifting-list-is-repaired-by-deleting-it-not-extending-it]]).
