# The measurement

**The region-coverage advisory read `9 of 15`, then `9 of 17`, across weeks — with nothing acted on.**
The numerator never moved. The denominator did, so the debt grew while the instrument reported it
faithfully every run.

**A contradiction pair has stood in the brief for six consecutive editions.** The journal of
2026-08-30 says six in its `Lessons` section and five in a feedback line filed earlier the same day;
**the discrepancy is left as it stands rather than picked**, because both are the session's own count
and the store does not correct a source's arithmetic. **Either way it is not one or two.**

# What it means

**An advisory with no decision about the debt behind it becomes decoration.** It is read, it is
accurate, and it changes nothing — because nobody owns the number and no ritual is obliged to move it.

**It does not mean advisories are useless.** It means **one is not a plan for a back-fill**. An
advisory answers *how much is owed*; it never answers *who pays it and when*, and those are the two
questions that make a debt shrink.

# What was done with it

**This measurement is why the body-section rule was scoped by a DATE rather than by a counter**
([[the-decision-lifecycle-fields-are-scoped-by-a-date-rather-than-back-filled]]). A dated cut needs
nobody to drive it to zero: the older cohort is exempt by rule, and every page written from the date
onwards is correct by construction.

**The alternative on the table was a counter over 299 pages**, and the argument against it was not
that it would be hard — it was that this store already has a live example of that exact instrument
being ignored for weeks.

# The general shape

**A number an instrument prints is evidence, not pressure.** If a count is supposed to fall, something
other than the count has to make it fall — a rule that forbids adding to it, a ritual that spends time
on it, or a date that makes the old population irrelevant. **Printing it harder does not work**, and
this store now has weeks of evidence for that.
