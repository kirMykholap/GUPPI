# Decided by the owner on 2026-08-18, as one policy rather than two calls

**A proposal that has been built is retired to `archive/` with `superseded_by:` — after the
librarian first checks whether anything on it lives nowhere else, and moves that onto a page.**

**Rejected: a call per page.** Two proposals were built on 2026-08-18 and the owner retired both
under one instruction, rather than weighing each. What is not delegated is the check: the retirement
is mechanical, the check before it is not.

## The test comes from the one page that was kept alive

[[marked-regions-and-the-prose-outside-them]] was built and **stayed `current`**, on the librarian's
reasoning that its layer-2 measurement — the imperative-word grep, 103 hits across ten files —
is described on no other page, and that retiring it would need a successor duplicating a concept
page rather than replacing it. The owner ruled again on 2026-08-18 that it stays.

**So the criterion is not *was it built*.** It is *does the page still hold something that would be
lost*. Both are needed: `proposals-are-a-folder-not-a-status` says a proposal is `deprecated` once
it is built or refused ([[proposals-are-a-folder-not-a-status]]), and this says what has to happen
first.

## What it obliges the librarian to do before the move

1. **Read the page whole against the corpus**, not just its title and its open questions.
2. **Move what lives only there onto a page** — a decision, a concept, or a lesson, whichever it is.
3. **Retire, with the retirement note naming where each part went.** Both retirements of 2026-08-18
   carry that table ([[the-marker-carries-a-type-and-governs-is-read-as-a-field]],
   [[the-map-in-claude-md-and-its-admission-test]]).

**The status field still cannot tell *built* from *refused***, which this does not fix
([[the-proposal-status-cannot-tell-built-from-refused]]). The retirement note is what carries that
distinction until it does.

# Retired 2026-08-22, on the owner's own words

**He ruled that a built proposal stays in `wiki/proposals/` with a `built` mark and does not move to
`archive/`, and that the ruling rewrites the ones that came before it**
([[a-built-proposal-stays-in-proposals-with-a-built-mark]]). This page states the move, so it is the
page that was rewritten.

**Nothing above is deleted, and the parts that survive are named** so a reader is not left guessing
which half was struck:

| what this page says | what became of it |
|---|---|
| a built proposal is retired to `archive/` with `superseded_by:` | **struck.** `superseded_by:` is a relation for a changed claim and a built proposal has no successor of that kind |
| a **refused** proposal retires | **untouched.** A refusal does have such a successor |
| read the page whole, and move what lives only there onto a page | **not carried forward as an obligation, and not struck.** There is no move for it to stand before, and nothing is lost if it is not done |
| the criterion is *does the page still hold something that would be lost*, not *was it built* | **still the right question**, and it is now asked of a page that stays where it is |
| `status` cannot tell built from refused | **unchanged and now half-answered** ([[the-proposal-status-cannot-tell-built-from-refused]]) |

**The retirements taken under this rule are not reversed here.** About thirty pages went to
`archive/` while it stood; whether any comes back is not in his words and is not a librarian's call.
