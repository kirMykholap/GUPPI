# Three claims, 2026-08-17, and all three were wrong

| the claim | what the file said |
|---|---|
| `vault-coverage.sh` skips the handoff template | it has **never mentioned** the template ([[a-journal-is-told-from-a-form-by-a-property-not-a-filename]]) |
| a supersession would be "one movement" | **twelve live pages** cite the page in question ([[the-handoff-template-maps-onto-the-categories]]) |
| a line had been written to `vault-feedback.md` | it had not |

All three were corrected inside the session. **The first two had been repeated to the owner before
anyone measured them.**

# Why the shape matters more than the three instances

Each claim came from **prose describing the thing** — a skill's own account of itself, a brief's
summary of a supersession — or from **the intention to do something**, read back as though it had
been done. Neither is the file.

**It is the same habit that produced the false titles this session spent the morning removing**: a
page said *five* while the check printed *seven*, because the number was carried from an earlier
sentence rather than re-read from the run ([[a-stated-count-drifts-from-the-list-it-heads]]).

# The discipline

- **Quote the file, or say that you have not read it.** *"N at `<commit>`"* has survived every check
  in this project; *"it is now N"* has failed every one.
- **An intention is not a record.** The vault has a folder for the difference
  ([[proposals-are-a-folder-not-a-status]]), and the failure it was created for is this one at page
  scale ([[an-intent-note-compiled-as-testimony-describes-things-nobody-built]]).
- **The retro is where they surfaced**, under *contradicted itself*, and not during the work
  ([[the-retro-step]]).
