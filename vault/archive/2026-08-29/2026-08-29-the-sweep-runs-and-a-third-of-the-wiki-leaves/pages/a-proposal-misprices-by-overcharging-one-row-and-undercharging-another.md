# One page, overcharging one row and undercharging another

[[the-marker-carries-a-type-and-governs-is-read-as-a-field]] priced the build of the mechanism
change. Measured against the code on 2026-08-18:

| What it said | What was true |
|---|---|
| the singular type word *"needs a pluralisation the check does not currently do anywhere"* | the check does exactly that mapping, at `scripts/vault-check.sh:176`, with one special case for `entitie` — and the inverse is that same line read backwards |
| the singular owes a rewriter-and-detector pass over the existing markers | **it owes none**: all nine live markers were already in the target form, so that row belonged to the *folder* spelling only |
| *"the drop of a gate"* — one item | **three branches** behind one condition, with three different correct fates ([[the-governs-branch-widens-and-the-others-stay-rules-only]]) |

**Executing its build table literally would have reddened the check.** Overcharging pushed the
owner toward the wrong spelling; undercharging would have broken the gate.

**Closed 2026-08-19: the rename landed and the name counts nothing.** The sentence above was

# What caught it, and what did not

**Both were caught at `/pre-task`, by reading the code.** Neither was caught by reading the
proposal — and the proposal had been read, approved and cited by four pages by then
([[a-pre-task-paid-for-itself-in-findings-nobody-would-have-made]]).

**This is the second proposal in this corpus to be wrong about its own subject**
([[a-proposal-can-be-wrong-about-the-hole-it-describes]]), and the shapes differ: that one was wrong
about the hole it described, this one about the cost of filling it. The instruction that comes out
of both is the same and is already on the record — **build from the code, not from the proposal**
([[a-proposal-must-be-rechecked-when-the-frame-changes]]).

# Why mispricing is specifically dangerous here

**A price is what a decision is made against.** A wrong claim in a proposal's prose gets argued
with; a wrong figure in its build table gets *chosen on*, silently, because the decision it feeds is
about which of two options is cheaper. The owner picked the singular anyway, on other grounds —
that is luck, not a mechanism.

**And the same failure shape has a measured cousin**: a stated count drifting from the list it heads
([[a-stated-count-drifts-from-the-list-it-heads]]). A build table is a stated count of work.
