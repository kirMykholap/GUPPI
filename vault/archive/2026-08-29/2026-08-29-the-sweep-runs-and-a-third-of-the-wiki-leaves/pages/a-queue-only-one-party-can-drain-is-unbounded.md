# The brief's `Alarms` section, as of 2026-08-17

**Three items stand there, two of them supersessions owed since 2026-08-16.** Every one of them can
be discharged by the owner and by nobody else: a supersession, a contradiction between two
`basis: human` pages, and a correction to the store's own rulebook are all judgements about the
project rather than about the store ([[the-librarian-writes-unattended]]).

**Nothing bounds the section.** The librarian appends to it at every ingest; only the owner
subtracts.

# Why that ends the same way every time

**An unbounded list that one person alone can shorten will stop being read** — which is not a
prediction, it is the failure the density advisories already demonstrate one level down: an alarm
that fires on every run and cannot be acted on is one you learn not to read
([[the-dense-tags-are-not-split-and-the-standing-advisories-are-named-here]]).

The brief is read at the start of **every** session, so the cost of an unread section there is paid
by every session rather than by the one that let it grow ([[the-growth-signal-is-the-brief]]).

# What it points at

- **The sprint is trying to close exactly this**, one level up: S1's criterion 1 asked that every
  lint finding carry an outcome, which is the same question asked of a different list.
- **A queue with no owner becomes a backlog nobody reads** was the ground for refusing an action
  queue in the retro ([[the-retro-has-no-action-queue]]) — and this section is the case where the
  owner *is* named and the list still grows.
- **Nothing here is the librarian's to resolve.** Recorded, raised in the brief, and left.
