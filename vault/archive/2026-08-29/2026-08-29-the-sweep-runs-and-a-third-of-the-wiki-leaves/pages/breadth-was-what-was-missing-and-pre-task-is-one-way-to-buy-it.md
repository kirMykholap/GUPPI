# The owner's theory, and the precise half of it that holds

**He proposed that the day's errors came from not running `/pre-task` and searching the wiki by
hand.** Tested against the two errors:

- **The `MIGRATION_CHECK_DATABASE_URL` hole supports it.** A targeted read of the pages the brief
  named could not find a page nobody pointed at
  ([[a-hole-was-shipped-in-work-already-reported-as-verified]]).
- **The `Find-Bash` error does not support it at all.** That is a fact about code, settled by one
  `grep -rn 'function Find-Bash'`, and no vault query would have held it
  ([[a-fact-about-code-was-generalised-from-its-neighbour]]).

# And the ritual would not have caught the one it explains

**`/pre-task` as specified enumerates `decisions/`**, and
[[migrations-do-not-drift-from-the-models]] is in `concepts/`. So the ritual, run exactly as
written, would have missed the page that held the answer.

**What was actually missing is BREADTH** — reading across folders rather than along the pointers a
brief supplies. `/pre-task` is one way to buy it. The read-only scouts are another, and they are
the one that demonstrably worked on this occasion
([[read-only-scouts-one-per-folder-ahead-of-the-librarian]]).

# Why the distinction is worth keeping precise

**A theory that is half right and adopted whole produces a ritual nobody believes in.** Attributing
a code-fact error to a missing vault query teaches the wrong repair for that error, and the right
repair — one grep before generalising — is cheaper than any ritual.

**The divergence stands on its own terms regardless.** `CLAUDE.md` requires `/pre-task` and it was
not run ([[pre-task-was-not-run-before-the-first-items-of-this-session]]); that a wider reading
would have been better still is a separate finding.
