# The ruling, 2026-08-26

*(the owner selected it, against downgrading such a page to `basis: inference`.)*

**A claim the owner ratified keeps `basis: human`, whatever voice it is written in.**

**`basis:` records where the assertion came from, not whose words recorded it.** A ruling he took is
`human` whether it reached the page as a quotation or as the session's summary of what he agreed to.
Downgrading it to `inference` would say *the librarian concluded this*, which is false about a
ratified ruling and would rank it below pages that merely quote him
([[a-mark-that-names-the-shape-of-a-decision-is-not-obliged-to-carry-the-words]]).

# What this does not weaken

**The signature rule is untouched.** A paraphrase may not be written inside an `owner:` block: where
a session understood rather than heard, it writes in its own voice and does not sign it
([[a-block-attributed-to-the-owner-is-written-in-his-own-russian]],
[[a-paraphrase-signed-owner-becomes-a-quote-and-is-reasoned-from-as-his-word]]).

**The two answer different questions.** The signature says *are these his words*; `basis:` says *who
decided this*. A page can honestly answer *no* to the first and `human` to the second, and it is
exactly that pair the ruling protects.

# Rejected

**Rejected: `basis: inference` for any page whose owner-attributed text is not verbatim.** It would
mark every ratified summary as the librarian's own conclusion, and `basis:` is the field conflict
resolution rests on — a page on `inference` cannot argue with a page on `human`.
