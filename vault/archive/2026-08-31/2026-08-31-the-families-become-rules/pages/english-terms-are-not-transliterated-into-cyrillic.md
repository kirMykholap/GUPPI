# The owner's instruction, 2026-08-18

> **owner: do not transliterate English technical terms into Cyrillic.** "vault", not «ваулт»;
> "proposal", not «пропозал».

**They are the repository's own vocabulary**, and a Cyrillic transliteration is neither the term he
can grep for nor a Russian word. It is written down because it governs **every future report to
him**, and a register instruction that lives only in one conversation is one nobody inherits.

## Where it sits relative to the language rule

The language rule decides *which language*: English for everything written into the repository,
Russian for chat with the owner and for what he sees in the tool
([[english-in-the-repository-russian-with-the-owner]],
[[english-is-the-rule-and-the-russian-past-is-deferred]]). **This decides how a term behaves inside
the Russian half** — the identifier stays in its own alphabet, the sentence around it is Russian.

**It is the same principle that keeps status values English inside a Russian interface**
([[statuses-stay-english-identifiers]]): a name that is stored, compared and searched is an
identifier and not copy, whatever language surrounds it.

## Why the grep argument is the load-bearing one

Retrieval here is `ripgrep` plus links, and the alias list exists so a Russian question reaches an
English page ([[aliases-carry-the-question-across-languages]]). A transliterated term defeats both
ends at once: it matches no page, and it is not the word an alias would be minted for. **Written as
«ваулт», the vault is unfindable in its own vault.**

## It renders — owner, 2026-08-18

**The instruction joins the language rule's slice** and reaches the repository's `CLAUDE.md` through
that rule's region: *"It is about how to write, and the language rule is the only place that is
read."* Written into [[english-in-the-repository-russian-with-the-owner]] at this ingest.

**This page keeps the reasoning and loses nothing.** A rendered rule carries the instruction; the
argument, the grep case and the relation to the status-identifier rule stay here, which is the
standing division between a rule and the decision behind it
([[what-travels-from-a-rule-page-is-a-marked-slice]], [[rules-becomes-a-type-of-its-own]]).
